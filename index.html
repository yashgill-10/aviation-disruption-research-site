Research Question and Scope

How do Flight/Weather and Airport/Network scores compare in ranking and classifying BTS departure delays at T−2h and T−30m on common 2024 flights, and how does a logistic stack of their continuous probabilities compare in the November–December exploratory evaluation?

This is a matched comparison of different pipelines, not a controlled feature ablation. Estimator families, training populations, feature engineering, and threshold selection periods differ across models.

Data, Identity, and Eligibility

The cleaned 2024 source contains 6,982,766 data rows. Canonical identity resolves 6,982,525 rows uniquely; 220 rows are ambiguous, 1 is identity-incomplete, and 20 are unmatched. These exceptions were preserved rather than assigned inferred IDs.

Data sources: U.S. BTS TranStats on-time-performance flight records (schedule fields for 2022–2023 training; 2024 development/validation) and NOAA/NCEI GHCNh hourly FM15 METAR observations mapped to BTS airport codes. See the BTS pipeline notes and the weather data plan.

For the retrospective cohorts, final BTS outcomes exclude cancelled flights and flights whose actual departure was already more than two hours before scheduled departure for T−2h, or more than 30 minutes before scheduled departure for T−30m. This corresponds to CANCELLED == 0 and DEP_DELAY > -120 minutes at T−2h, and CANCELLED == 0 and DEP_DELAY > -30 minutes at T−30m.

Because cancellation and actual departure are final outcomes, these eligibility rules describe retrospective research cohorts; they do not establish which flights would have been eligible using information available live at either cutoff.

39 ambiguous October–December rows were scored but excluded from canonical matched comparison due to no defensible canonical ID.

Data Preprocessing and Feature Construction

The workflow is chronological and keeps the target separate from prediction-time inputs.

  1. Monthly sourcesBTS on-time records and NOAA/NCEI GHCNh FM15 METAR observations.
  2. Validate and cleanCheck archive CRC, source month and flight date; audit exclusions and build monthly Parquet.
  3. Resolve identityAssign canonical IDs only where the nine-field BTS schedule identity is unambiguous.
  4. Build featuresCreate separate T−2h and T−30m Flight/Weather and Airport/Network inputs.
  5. Score componentsProduce saved continuous Flight/Weather and Airport/Network probabilities.
  6. Fit M3Standardize component-score logits and train the logistic stack on September rows.
  7. Evaluate retrospectivelyKeep each comparison period and its cohort denominator separate.

BTS archive processing and target

Monthly BTS TranStats ZIP archives are integrity-checked, validated against their source month and each record’s flight date, then processed into audited Parquet partitions. The 2024 monthly audit counted 7,079,061 source rows. Excluding 96,315 cancelled rows left 6,982,746 eligible modelling rows. The audit also counted 92,970 rows with missing DepDel15; the missing-label rows are contained within the cancelled rows and should not be added as a separate exclusion. This is distinct from the 6,982,766-row cleaned identity source described below. Cancelled flights and records with missing or invalid binary DepDel15 labels are excluded from the supervised table. DepDel15 is the outcome, not a feature. Actual departure or arrival, delay minutes or causes, cancellation outcomes, and other post-departure fields are not predictors for that same flight.

Partitions and identity

2022–2023 is the training period; 2024 is development and validation. The 2025 archive partition is complete but was opened for an earlier frozen schedule/history baseline comparison, so it is not a blind final test of the integrated work. A verified local Flight/Weather runtime handoff contains separate January–July 2026 operational-history files for inference lookbacks only. The claimed August 2026 update was not present in the reviewed archive/Parquet handoff and is not asserted here.

The cleaned 2024 identity source has 6,982,766 rows, of which 6,982,525 resolve uniquely; 220 are ambiguous, one identity is incomplete, and 20 are unmatched. bts-flight-v1 is a SHA-256 identity over nine normalized schedule fields: date, reporting-airline DOT ID, reporting airline, reporting flight number, origin airport ID/code, destination airport ID/code, and scheduled departure time. It supports stable cross-workstream joins. Ambiguous, incomplete and unmatched rows remain without inferred IDs. These counts describe source identity resolution, not the matched evaluation cohort.

Flight/Weather features

Scheduled-flight features are combined with strictly prior BTS delay history: three prior completed calendar months and prior 7-day/30-day completed-day windows, with global, airline, origin, route and origin-hour aggregates where defined. Daily windows exclude the target flight’s date, and sparse-group rates use a 50-flight weighted backoff to the matching global rate. NOAA/NCEI GHCNh FM15 METAR observations are linked through the airport-to-station map. Historical joins require observation time plus a 15-minute availability proxy to be at or before the flight-specific cutoff; reports older than three hours are treated as missing and missingness is represented explicitly. The proxy is not an original receipt/ingestion timestamp.

Airport/Network features

The Airport/Network feature set includes scheduled-flight information, prior-seven-day airport aggregates, scheduled origin departures in [scheduled departure − 2 hours, scheduled departure), and recent origin activity reconstructed from final BTS outcomes. The schedule count is anchored to scheduled departure and shared by both horizons. Schedule-vintage and BTS event-availability timestamps are unavailable; retrospective feature values do not establish operational availability by either cutoff.

M3 stacking

  1. Clip each continuous component probability to [1e−6, 1 − 1e−6].
  2. Transform the clipped probabilities into logits.
  3. Standardize the logits with a scaler fitted on September 2024 rows.
  4. Fit an L2 Logistic Regression stacker on September.
  5. Select the operating threshold on October by maximizing F1.
  6. Evaluate on November–December 2024 without refitting.

The exact row-level September M3 training-ID list was not saved. See the BTS pipeline, weather plan, canonical identity notes, and Airport/Network schema.

Model Methodology and Live Inference Status

Baseline: A schedule-only DepDel15 baseline (airline, route, scheduled times, distance, elapsed time; logistic SGD with Platt calibration and F1 threshold selection on January–June 2024) was developed as documented in docs/baseline_model.md. Its metrics are local artifacts not republished on this page; an earlier exploratory 2025 check is classified as descriptive only, not a blind test.

Flight/Weather Models

Training: SGDClassifier trained on 2022–2023. Platt LogisticRegression calibrator and F1 threshold selected on January–June 2024. September scores used saved classifier and calibrator without refitting.

Features: Schedule fields, historical delay rates (prior 3 months, 7/30 days), and GHCNh FM15 METAR observations with 15-minute availability proxy and 180-minute staleness limit.

Live Inference Status: Historical evaluation complete. Local inference prototype exists via src/flight_weather_web.py and src/predict_current_flights.py for prediction-time schedule data with prior BTS history and available decoded METARs.

Limitations: Complete estimator hyperparameter, feature-selection, and model-family selection history not recovered. Weather uses observation-time proxy, not actual receipt timestamps. Schedule-vintage and BTS publication timestamps unavailable.

Airport/Network Models

Training: RandomForestClassifier (50 trees, max depth 12, min leaf size 10, class_weight="balanced_subsample", random state 42). Stratified sample up to 200,000 eligible January–August 2024 flights per horizon. September used to choose operating threshold by maximizing F1.

Features: Airport network state, recent activity reconstructed from final BTS outcomes, schedule information.

Live Inference Status: Historical evaluation complete. Live inference is not validated; original scoring-time feature builder not recovered.

Limitations: Original scoring-time feature builder not recovered. Recorded builder SHA-256 a77c70db... identifies unrecovered source; commonly cited c107a5eb... is the scoring runner, not the builder. Complete model selection history not recovered.

M3 Logistic Stack

Architecture: Logistic Regression (C=1, solver="lbfgs", max_iter=1000) on two logit-transformed, standardized continuous component probabilities.

Protocol: September 2024 meta-training, October 2024 threshold selection (maximize F1), November–December 2024 evaluation. StandardScaler fit on September rows only. Probabilities clipped to [1e-6, 1−1e-6] before logit transformation.

Live Inference Status: Retrospective stacking evaluation complete. Live inference is not validated; requires both Flight/Weather and Airport/Network component scores for new predictions.

Reproducibility: The actual retrospective training/evaluation script is published in the source repository under m3/; rerunning it requires the private component score exports, which are not distributed. Trained models and row-level scores remain private; their SHA-256 hashes are recorded in m3/m3_run_manifest.json.

Limitations: Exact row-level September training-ID list not saved. M3 model files serialized after evaluation predictions calculated; pre-evaluation serialized-model hash not established.

Model Development Progression

  1. Schedule-only baseline

    Airline, route, scheduled times, distance and scheduled elapsed time establish a simple starting point.

  2. Historical delay features

    Strictly prior global and grouped BTS delay history adds context; evaluated separately on July–December 2024.

  3. Flight/Weather horizons

    METAR observations are added and compared at T−2h and T−30m in a distinct weather ablation.

  4. Airport/Network

    Airport state and recent activity form a separate component pipeline, matched against Flight/Weather in October–December.

  5. M3 logistic stack

    Continuous component probabilities are stacked using September meta-training, October threshold selection, and November–December evaluation.

These stages use distinct experiments and populations. The July–December weather ablation, October–December component comparison, November–December M3 evaluation, and earlier 2025 baseline exploration are not one performance table. The 2025 baseline comparison is not a blind integrated-model test.

Results Interpretation

October–December 2024 component comparison

At T−2h, Flight/Weather average precision was 0.301968 and Airport/Network was 0.296262. At T−30m, Flight/Weather was 0.301948 and Airport/Network was 0.313754. This is a matched comparison of different pipelines, not a controlled feature ablation.

November–December 2024 M3 evaluation

Average precision was 0.327948 / 0.322279 / 0.344739 for Flight/Weather, Airport/Network, and M3 at T−2h, and 0.327614 / 0.342772 / 0.357079 at T−30m. M3 also had higher ROC-AUC and recall and lower (better) Brier scores than both components at both horizons, but lower precision. F1 and balanced accuracy were mixed: M3 was slightly below Flight/Weather at T−2h and above both components at T−30m. These are descriptive point estimates, with no statistical-significance claim.

Error Analysis (Aggregate)

Retrospective October–December 2024 Airport/Network analysis (report; aggregate tables under error_analysis/): at the recorded thresholds the predicted-positive rate (~37–40%) far exceeds the observed 16.5% delay rate, and ten-bin ECE is high (~27–28%), so scores are best treated as uncalibrated ranking signals rather than likelihoods. Recall is descriptive-only across months (higher in December), carriers, and origins, with minimum-group-size guards and Wilson intervals in the aggregate tables. Full details and caveats are in the linked report; ordinal-keyed aggregates predate canonical-ID joining.

Threats to Validity and Limitations

  • Provenance: Airport/Network original scoring-time builder not recovered. M3 row-level September training-ID list not saved. Complete model selection histories for all three models not recovered.
  • Feature availability: Schedule-vintage timestamps, BTS publication/ingestion times, and recent airport-state availability timestamps unavailable. Flight/Weather uses 15-minute weather observation-time proxy, not actual receipt timestamps.
  • Evaluation status: October–December 2024 and 2025 have been inspected; neither is a blind final test. 2025 cannot serve as an untouched holdout.
  • Threshold differences: Component thresholds selected in different periods (Flight/Weather Jan–Jun, Airport/Network Sep); M3 threshold selected October. Procedures not identical.
  • Matched comparison only: Different pipelines compared on matched IDs; differences cannot be attributed exclusively to feature additions.
  • Live inference: Flight/Weather has a local inference prototype; Airport/Network and M3 live inference are not validated. This website displays stored results only.
  • No deployment claims: Results do not establish real-time feature availability, operational readiness, causal effects, or deployment validity.

Conclusion

The saved retrospective results show that the M3 logistic stack ranked delayed flights better by average precision and captured more delayed flights by recall than either component on the November–December 2024 matched sample. Precision was lower, and threshold-dependent results varied by horizon. These findings are exploratory, not a blind final-test result. Builder provenance, feature availability, selection history, and a genuinely untouched test period remain unresolved. No live-readiness or passenger-service claim is made.
